// OVERVIEW

Sonic the Hedgehog is a platformer built around speed. Sonic races through loops, slopes and springs, collecting rings that protect him from damage while he tries to stop Doctor Robotnik.

Levels are designed with several routes at different heights. Fast, skilful play keeps Sonic on the higher, quicker paths, while mistakes drop him onto slower and more dangerous ones.

// HISTORY

Sonic the Hedgehog was developed by Sonic Team and released by Sega for the Mega Drive, known as the Genesis in North America, in 1991. Programmer Yuji Naka, character designer Naoto Ohshima and level designer Hirokazu Yasuhara were central to its creation.

Sega created Sonic as a mascot that could compete with Nintendo's Mario. The game's fast pace and attitude became central to Sega's marketing during the 16-bit console rivalry of the early 1990s.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Sonic the Hedgehog showed that a platformer could be built around speed and flow rather than careful, precise jumps.

It gave Sega a mascot that defined the company's identity and became one of the best-known characters in video games.
