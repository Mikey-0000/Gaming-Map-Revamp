// OVERVIEW

Myst is a first-person puzzle adventure set on a mysterious island. The player arrives with no instructions and explores by clicking through a series of detailed still images.

There are no enemies and no way to fail. Progress comes from observation: reading journals, noticing symbols and working out how the island's machines connect. Books on the island act as links to other worlds, called Ages.

// HISTORY

Myst was developed by Cyan, the studio of brothers Rand and Robyn Miller, and released for the Apple Macintosh in 1993, followed by Windows versions. Its world was built from pre-rendered 3D images that looked far more detailed than real-time graphics of the time.

The game arrived as CD-ROM drives were becoming common in home computers, and it was one of the titles that showed what the new format could hold. It became one of the best-selling PC games of the 1990s.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Myst showed that atmosphere and quiet discovery could carry a commercially successful game, without action or a fail state.

Its success helped establish the CD-ROM as a games format and influenced the exploration and puzzle games that followed, including many modern narrative exploration games.
