// OVERVIEW

Portal is a first-person puzzle game set inside the Aperture Science testing facility. The player, Chell, is given a device that creates two linked portals on flat surfaces. Anything that enters one portal comes out of the other.

Each test chamber is a puzzle about space and momentum. Players redirect objects, cross gaps and use falling speed to launch themselves, while an artificial intelligence called GLaDOS comments on their progress.

// HISTORY

Portal grew out of Narbacular Drop, a student project made at the DigiPen Institute of Technology. Valve hired members of the student team, who developed the portal idea into a full game.

Portal was released in 2007 as part of The Orange Box, a collection that also contained Half-Life 2 and Team Fortress 2. Although it was the shortest game in the package, it became one of the most talked-about releases of the year.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Portal proved that a short, tightly designed game could stand beside much larger releases, and that puzzles could be told through physics and space.

Its dry, darkly comic writing showed how a single character's voice could carry an entire story, and it is often cited as an example of teaching players through level design rather than tutorials.
