// OVERVIEW

Pac-Man is an arcade maze game. The player guides Pac-Man around a maze, eating dots while four ghosts chase him. Eating a power pellet briefly turns the ghosts blue so Pac-Man can eat them for points.

Each ghost follows its own chasing behaviour, so the group does not simply move together. Learning those behaviours lets skilled players plan routes and escape tight situations.

// HISTORY

Pac-Man was designed by Toru Iwatani at Namco and released in Japanese arcades in 1980. Iwatani wanted a game that would appeal to a wider audience than the space shooters that dominated arcades at the time.

The game became a worldwide hit. Pac-Man was one of the first video game characters to become a pop-culture icon, appearing on merchandise, in songs and in a television cartoon.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Pac-Man showed that video games could appeal to a broad audience and that a character could be as important as the gameplay.

Its ghosts are an early, well-known example of giving enemies distinct personalities through simple behaviour rules.
