// OVERVIEW

Super Mario Bros. is a side-scrolling platformer in which Mario travels through the Mushroom Kingdom to rescue Princess Toadstool from Bowser. The player runs, jumps on enemies and collects power-ups across eight worlds.

The game's controls feel precise and physical. Mario speeds up gradually, carries momentum through jumps and can be steered in the air, which gives skilled players a great deal of control.

// HISTORY

Super Mario Bros. was developed by Nintendo and released for the Famicom in Japan in 1985, then for the Nintendo Entertainment System in North America. It was directed by Shigeru Miyamoto, with Takashi Tezuka as a key designer.

In North America the game was often packaged with the NES, and it played a major part in the recovery of the home console market after the video game crash of 1983.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Super Mario Bros. defined the side-scrolling platformer and set expectations for how a character should move and feel under the player's control.

Its first level is often studied as an example of teaching game rules through design alone, without written instructions.
