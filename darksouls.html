// OVERVIEW

Dark Souls is an action role-playing game set in Lordran, a decaying kingdom. Combat is deliberate and dangerous: every attack, dodge and block uses stamina, and even ordinary enemies can be deadly.

When the player dies they drop their souls, which are used to level up, and must reach the spot where they fell to recover them. Bonfires act as checkpoints, but resting at one brings most defeated enemies back.

// HISTORY

Dark Souls was developed by FromSoftware and directed by Hidetaka Miyazaki. It followed Demon's Souls, released in 2009, and kept that game's difficulty and online features.

It was released for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in 2011, and a PC version followed in 2012. Its success led to sequels and inspired many other studios to make games in a similar style.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Dark Souls influenced a generation of action games so strongly that games with similar design are often called 'soulslike'.

Its interconnected world and minimal, environmental storytelling showed that players would dig deeply into a game that trusted them to work things out.
